# Dungeons on Automatic Monster Library 0.6.0

A tagging and prose release. Every record's tags now come from a closed, documented vocabulary; every
description in the independently authored half is rewritten; and the notes a GM reads on the sheet are cut
down to the ones that change what happens at the table.

**This release renames a published field and retires 46 tags.** Both are breaking for a consumer that stored
either, and both are mapped below.

Original GURPS conversion by Enraged Eggplant, from *Monsters* (May 11, 2024); adapted and republished with
permission. Independently authored records by Monsters on Automatic. Released under
[CC BY 4.0](../../licenses/CC-BY-4.0.txt) with mandatory originator credit.

## What was wrong

Tags are the only part of a record a consumer filters on without reading the stat block, and they were worth
as much as they were consistent — which was not much.

- **54 of 175 tags named exactly one record.** `pod`, `pride`, `haunt`, `illusionist`, `draft`, `healer`,
  `friendly`, `primitive`. A filter that matches one record is not a filter.
- **Near-duplicates split single ideas in half**: `caster` and `spellcaster`, `night` and `nocturnal`, `town`
  and `urban`, `cave` and `underground`, `hunter` and `predator`.
- **`demon` and `devil` matched nothing**, while nine tanar'ri and eight baatezu sat in the library under
  names only a reader of the source would think to type. `celestial` matched one record while ten archons,
  angels, guardinals and eladrin sat beside it.
- **`wandering-encounter` was the second-most-used tag and carried no information.** Every record has a
  non-zero `encounter.wanderingWeight`, so the tag excluded nothing — and it was applied arbitrarily on top of
  that, sitting on Hunter Shark but not Reef Shark, Wolf but not Werewolf, every swarm but Spider Swarm, and
  on no humanoid, dragon or undead at all.
- **Nineteen records carried no environment tag**, including every horse and every dinosaur.

## The controlled vocabulary

[`scripts/srd/tag-taxonomy.mjs`](../../scripts/srd/tag-taxonomy.mjs) is now the closed catalogue, and the
build fails on a tag it does not define. Two rules decide what belongs:

- A **lineage** tag names a kind — `lich`, `drow`, `demon` — and may be the only one of its kind, because it
  still answers a question when the second one arrives.
- A **descriptive** tag is a filter, so it covers two or more records or is a documented wildcard
  (`any-terrain`).

Specific lineage tags entail broader ones. `baatezu` entails `devil` entails `fiend`; `archon`, `angel`,
`guardinal` and `eladrin` all entail `celestial`. Records state both, so `"is this a fiend"` is answerable
without the consumer holding a lookup table.

| | 0.5.1 | 0.6.0 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| distinct tags | 189 | 166 |
| tags naming a single record | 54 | 2 |
| `demon` / `devil` / `fiend` / `celestial` | 0 / 1 / 0 / 0 | 9 / 8 / 18 / 11 |
| `good`, against `evil` at 66 | 2 | 32 |
| records with no environment tag | 19 | 0 |

The two remaining single-record tags are `mercenary` and `spellcaster`. Both have their second and third
records among the nineteen independently authored drafts still held back pending review, and both stop being
singletons when those promote.

Where a tag restates something the build already says, it is now derived from the build and checked against
it: `flier` follows Flight, `stealthy` follows Silence, `burrower` follows Tunneling, `berserk` follows
Berserk, and `talker` follows the absence of Cannot Speak at IQ 6 or better. That pass found Oni and Ghost
flying without a `flier` tag.

## Breaking changes

### `size.massKg` is now `size.massLb`

The library is US customary throughout, which is also what GURPS Basic Lift and the published size table read
in. The figure is documentary — it is the body weight a build was scaled from, and nothing derives from it —
so **no ST, Size Modifier or DR changed with it.** Values are rounded to figures a US author would have
written rather than exact conversions of round metric ones.

153 records carry it. A consumer reading `size.massKg` will find it absent.

### Retired tags

46 tags are retired. `RETIRED_TAGS` in the taxonomy module carries the full table with the reason for each;
the mapping is:

| Retired | Now | Retired | Now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `caster` | `spellcaster` | `night` | `nocturnal` |
| `cave` | `underground` | `town` | `urban` |
| `highland` | `mountain` | `deep-water` | `open-water` |
| `hunter` | `predator` | `pod`, `pride` | `pack` |
| `fiendish`, `infernal` | `fiend` | `shapeshifter` | `shapechanger` |
| `familiar-scale` | `familiar` | `poisonous` | `poison` |
| `tanar-ri` | keeps, and adds `demon` | `baatezu` | keeps, and adds `devil` |
| `colour-*` | `color-*` | `true` | `dragon` |
| `spirit` | `incorporeal` | `haunt` | `tomb` |
| `just`, `kindly`, `noble` | `good` | `talkative` | `talker` |
| `draft` | `beast-of-burden` | `riding` | `mount` |
| `companion` | `domestic` | `ranger` | `scout` |

Dropped outright, with the note explaining what carries the meaning instead: `wandering-encounter`, `rare` and
`horde` (all three restated a number the record already publishes), `omnivore` (marked the *absence* of a
Restricted Diet trait), `beast`, `wilderness`, `warm`, `friendly`, `healer`, `illusionist`, `arcane`,
`scholar`, `primitive`.

## Stat notes

`stats.notes` renders on the GCS sheet and on the Foundry actor, so it is read mid-encounter. It now has to
change what happens at the table. Of 288 distinct notes on the authored records, 44 stayed:

- what actually works against the creature — *"A carapace is shell rather than hide. A scratch that would slip
  past thick skin does not slip past this."*
- what the stat block understates — *"Mail and a shield are standard issue and are not in this record's DR, so
  a hobgoblin met in the field is better protected than the stat block says."*
- what is deliberately not on the stat block — *"The enlargement a duergar can call is not on the stat block.
  It changes size, Strength and reach for a few minutes, and running it is the GM's job."*

Everything that justified a number moved to `provenance.conversionNotes`: why a record sits on the SM +2 row,
where its ST came from, which trait was bought at what price, which batch authored it. All of it is still
published, in the field an auditor reads. Nothing was deleted — including the outside-comparison disclosure
`ROADMAP.md` rule 6 requires, which travels in full.

A wolf's stat block went from eleven notes to two.

## Prose

Every description in the independently authored half is rewritten. The old ones all ran the same three beats
and read as one voice repeating itself. The prose is now third person throughout, US-spelled, and in US
customary units.

## What did not change

No attribute, trait, skill, attack, encounter field, damage figure or rating moved on any of the 457 records.
The published `.gct` and `.gcs` downloads change only where a citation line or a stat note does.
